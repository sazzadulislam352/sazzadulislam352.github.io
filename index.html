Biography

Sazzadul Islam is a data scientist and researcher based in Washington, DC, whose work applies machine learning and big data analytics to risk, compliance and decision-making problems in the banking and healthcare sectors. He earned his M.S. in Big Data Analytics from Bay Atlantic University, graduating Summa Cum Laude with a 3.94 GPA, and received the university's Presidential Award in 2024.

Before his graduate studies, he spent four years in corporate finance and accounting in Bangladesh, where he managed financial reporting, internal audits, budgeting and regulatory tax compliance. This practical grounding in how financial institutions measure and report risk informs the applied focus of his research.

He has authored or co-authored eight peer-reviewed journal articles, five as first or sole author, which together have been cited 170 times. He is preparing to pursue a Doctor of Computer Science, with a research agenda in trustworthy machine learning for regulated industries.

Research Statement

Banks, regulators and healthcare providers increasingly rely on automated models to make consequential decisions: whether a bank holds enough liquidity to survive a shock, whether a transaction is suspicious, whether a borrower is creditworthy, or how a clinician should act on patient data. These models are often accurate but opaque, and many are not built for the volume and speed of modern data.

My research addresses this gap. I design machine learning and deep learning methods that operate at big-data scale while remaining interpretable and auditable, so that their outputs can be trusted by risk officers, compliance teams, examiners and clinicians. My work spans three connected threads: (1) deep learning for financial stress testing and credit risk under regulatory frameworks such as Basel III; (2) machine learning for anti-money-laundering and anomaly detection in large financial data pipelines; and (3) real-time human-AI decision support and quality automation for US healthcare systems.

Looking ahead, I aim to develop explainable, regulation-aware learning systems that can be deployed in supervised industries, and to study how such systems can be evaluated for reliability, fairness and compliance before they are trusted with high-stakes decisions.
